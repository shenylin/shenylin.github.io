      Applied Research · Service Platform

    <h1 class="mb-3">
      Designing a Virtual Partnership for Students, Teachers, and University Tutors
    </h1>
    <p class="lead mb-4">
      How might a statewide platform connect high school students and teachers
      with tutoring, professional development, and university resources—without
      compromising student safety?
    </p>

    <dl class="row research-meta mb-4">
      <dt class="col-sm-3">Client</dt>
      <dd class="col-sm-9">Office of the Chancellor, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign</dd>

      <dt class="col-sm-3">Role</dt>
      <dd class="col-sm-9">Design Consultant, Illinois Design Consulting</dd>

      <dt class="col-sm-3">Duration</dt>
      <dd class="col-sm-9">June 2022–May 2023</dd>

      <dt class="col-sm-3">Methods</dt>
      <dd class="col-sm-9">Secondary research, benchmarking, 16 interviews, synthesis, personas, journey mapping, prototyping, card sorting, and concept testing</dd>
    </dl>
  </header>

  <section class="research-section">
    <h2>Context and challenge</h2>
    <p>
      The Illinois Virtual Partnership began as a response to a problem made
      visible by the pandemic. High school students experiencing learning loss
      lacked a centralized way to get help, while teachers navigating the same
      disruption had no easy way to connect with UIUC resources or with one
      another.
    </p>
    <p>
      The Office of the Chancellor set out to explore a single platform that
      would combine high-impact tutoring with professional development for
      teachers, building on the university's existing outreach infrastructure.
      I joined the year-long engagement as a Design Consultant on a
      cross-functional student consulting team working with product designers,
      project managers, and faculty advisors.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-section research-contribution">
    <h2>My contribution</h2>
    <ul>
      <li>I participated in all 16 stakeholder interviews and worked with two faculty advisors to synthesize the findings into six design insights.</li>
      <li>I helped translate those insights into personas, journey maps, and concepts prototyped in Figma.</li>
      <li>I served as the student lead for the faculty-directed concept-testing evaluation.</li>
      <li>I helped investigate safety patterns in comparable platforms and translate them into verification and reporting concepts.</li>
      <li>I joined the project managers in presenting the safety findings to the client's legal team.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section class="research-section">
    <h2>Research approach</h2>
    <p>
      We began by benchmarking four existing UIUC and statewide outreach
      programs: the Principal's Scholars Program, WYSE, TRIO Upward Bound, and
      Illinois Virtual School. This helped us understand which resources and
      relationships could be connected rather than rebuilt.
    </p>
    <p>
      A faculty advisor then led 16 semi-structured interviews with high school
      students, parents, teachers, UIUC tutors and faculty, and a school
      counselor. I participated in every session and worked with two faculty
      advisors to synthesize the research. Six key insights—including student
      autonomy, teacher burnout, the value of small customizations, and the
      opportunity to strengthen teacher–UIUC relationships—shaped the design
      direction.
    </p>
    <p>
      We developed personas for a teacher, student, and student tutor, then
      mapped their journeys through concepts prototyped in Figma. These included
      a short-form feed of tutoring content, tutor matching and chat, and
      individualized progress dashboards.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-section">
    <h2>Concept testing revealed a larger risk</h2>
    <p>
      I served as the student lead for a month-long, faculty-directed concept
      evaluation. Each 50-minute session combined card sorting with prototype
      walkthroughs involving subject-matter experts, teachers, and a student.
    </p>
    <p>
      The six earlier insights each informed a feature: video-based content for
      familiar browsing behavior, badges and dashboards that encouraged
      engagement without removing student autonomy, and shared channels that
      could strengthen teacher communities. But the evaluation surfaced
      something more consequential. Every subject-matter expert and teacher we
      spoke with raised concerns about student safety without being prompted.
    </p>
    <p>
      Because the platform would connect minors directly with adult tutors,
      participants wanted clear answers about content moderation, tutor
      verification, parent visibility, and reporting. The pattern moved the
      project beyond what interface design alone could resolve.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-section">
    <h2>Turning safety concerns into actionable decisions</h2>
    <p>
      We examined how platforms such as Airbnb, Bumble, and Uber support trust
      between strangers through identity verification, secure and monitored
      messaging, safety guidance, and clear reporting paths. We then asked how
      the platform might improve safety without undermining the student
      autonomy identified in the earlier research.
    </p>
    <p>
      We proposed a tutor-verification protocol and an in-app reporting system.
      Alongside the project managers, I brought the research to the client's
      legal team so they could assess what Illinois student privacy law would
      require before the concepts could move forward.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-section">
    <h2>What I carried forward</h2>
    <p>
      This project showed me that research does not always culminate in a
      feature recommendation. Sometimes its most useful outcome is identifying
      a risk clearly enough to involve the people equipped to resolve it. I
      left the engagement in May 2023 while the legal questions were still
      under review, so I do not claim that the proposed platform ultimately
      launched.
    </p>
  </section>
