Hello, I'm Cheng-Hsuan (Sheny) Lin.

My current research asks how people perceive support and understanding from AI systems. This question took shape during my graduate work at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, analyzing data from a study on how older adults perceive conversational AI, later published at CSCW 2024. In my own analysis, I found an interaction effect I hadn't expected — a moment that convinced me research insight belongs at the center of product decisions, and that the people who stand to benefit most are often the ones systems are least built for: older adults, children, and other cognitively vulnerable populations. (2021–present)

Before that, at National Chiao Tung University, I independently designed and ran an experiment on how advertising effectiveness shifts across social and individual TV viewing, later published in the International Journal of Communication (2021). Each of these has been a variation on the same question: how do we measure what a person perceives? I've been asking it since I entered social science research in 2015.

I hold master's degrees in Information Management from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (2023) and Communication Studies from National Chiao Tung University (2018).
